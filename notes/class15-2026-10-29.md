## Supply and demand fundamentals
- **Movement along the demand curve vs a shift of the demand curve:**
  - **Movement along demand:** *supply* is shifting.
  - **Shift in demand:** something external changes (income, a festival on the green).
- **Demand shock u:** the error term that shifts the demand intercept.
  - Positive u: the intercept rises and the curve shifts right.
  - Negative u: the curve shifts left.
- **In equilibrium, price is a function of u**, so P is correlated with the error term.
  - Any unobservable in the supply or demand system moves price around.
  - Supply shocks (v) also feed through price into both curves.

## The endogeneity problem
- With only P and Q data you **can't tell a supply shift from a demand shift**. Infinitely many supply/demand combinations produce the same two points.
- Regressing Q on P often gives an **upward-sloping demand curve**, which is nonsense.
  - **More data doesn't fix endogeneity.** It gives a more precise estimate of the wrong thing.
  - **Rental car pricing example:** ignoring inventory vs demand shocks gives garbage results.
- The goal: isolate *movement along* the demand curve from *shifts of* the demand curve.

## Instrumental variables and 2SLS
- **An instrument must be:**
  - **Relevant:** it moves the equilibrium price (e.g. flour cost).
  - **Excluded:** it moves price only through the supply curve, never demand directly.
  - **Independent:** it is unrelated to the demand shock u.
- **The exclusion restriction is the slippery one.** You have to reason through the spillbacks:
  - **Wages:** higher staff wages raise workers' incomes, which raises pizza demand, so exclusion is violated.
  - **East Rock taxes:** lower taxes cut costs but also raise neighborhood incomes. Same problem.
- A cost shock shifts supply, which causes movement along demand, which lets you estimate the demand curve.
- **The 2SLS procedure:** regress P on the instruments Z → predict P̂ → regress Q on P̂.
  - **First stage:** regress equilibrium price on the instrument(s) Z and save the fitted values P̂.
  - **Second stage:** regress Q on P̂, plus any exogenous controls W such as income.
  - P̂ keeps only the price variation driven by supply shocks and strips out the endogenous demand variation.
  - **Visually:** it collapses the messy P–Q scatter into clean demand-curve variation.
- **Multiple instruments:** put all the Zs in the first stage; put exogenous controls (income) in **both** stages.
- **Invalid instruments bias the estimate.** One strong valid instrument beats adding weak ones.
- **Supply works the same way:** use demand-side instruments (e.g. income in Hamden).
- **Python:** the `linearmodels` IV module. The formula syntax puts brackets around the endogenous price and its instruments, e.g. `Q ~ 1 + W + [P ~ Z]`.
