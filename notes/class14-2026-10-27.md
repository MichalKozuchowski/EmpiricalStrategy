## Regression discontinuity (RD)
- **Idea:** exploit a cutoff where something changes systematically above vs below.
- **Yelp:**
  - Ratings are rounded in 0.25 steps (an average of 3.75 shows as 4.0), so the cutoff isolates the causal effect of displayed stars on demand.
  - Demand boosts of **20%, 23% and 11%** crossing the successive thresholds.
- **Superfund** (Greenstone & Gallagher), cutoff 28.5:
  - Above the cutoff a site is eligible for cleanup funding; below it isn't.
  - The property-value effect is **consistently negative but insignificant across bandwidths 3, 6, 9, 12, 16**.
  - The bandwidth changes the point estimate, not the significance.
- **Validity threats:**
  - **Endogenous sorting:** businesses buying fake reviews to cross 3.75.
  - **Confounders at the cutoff:** e.g. being featured on the homepage at exactly 3.75.
  - **Omitted variables:** the positive slope at high hazard scores may reflect a concentration of California sites.
- **Sharp vs fuzzy RD:** sharp = the cutoff fully determines treatment; fuzzy = treatment is only more likely past the cutoff (e.g. Superfund listing).

## Difference-in-differences (DiD)
- **2×2 design:** a control group (ads on throughout) vs a treated group (ads on, then off). Example: western CT = control, eastern CT = treatment.
- **Estimate** = the change in treated minus the change in control. The naive before/after is −$0.40; netting out the control trend gives **−$0.80**.
- **Parallel trends:** the control group must represent what would have happened to the treated group without treatment. You can check it in the pre-period, but it's ultimately an assumption.
  - **Court example:** battery prices vs light-bulb prices as a control. It failed because lithium input costs spiked independently.
- **A violated assumption can miss the true effect by a factor of 3 or more.**

## Two-way fixed effects (TWFE)
- Extends DiD to richer data: 80 CT towns × months.
  - **Place FE** absorb town-level differences.
  - **Time FE** absorb monthly movements common to all towns.
- **Advantages over the basic 2×2:**
  - easy to add covariates;
  - handles **staggered rollout** (towns losing ads at different times).
- An active research area: hundreds of recent papers on TWFE problems (endogenous timing, etc.). Amazon has hired many of the academics working on these methods.

## Synthetic control
- **Question:** what would New Haven's revenue have been if the ads had never stopped?
- **Method:** the weighted mix of control towns that best reconstructs New Haven's pre-treatment series. The weights minimize squared loss (like OLS). Branford 59.2%, Cheshire ~20%, the rest spread across other towns.
- **Validation (placebo):**
  - Run SC on untreated towns; their "effects" should be about 0.
  - Towns with big spurious effects can signal **data-quality problems**.
- Can add covariates (income, population density, vacancy, college presence).
- **vs DiD/TWFE:** the same question under different assumptions, so **run both as a robustness check.**
