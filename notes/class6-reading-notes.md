# Class 6 reading — Bui & Kliff, "Air Travel Is Already Back to Normal in Some Places"
**The New York Times (The Upshot), published Apr 1, 2021, updated Sept 7, 2021.** Data and
analysis credited to **Kevin Williams** (the course's professor) using TSA checkpoint data — the
exact same dataset used in [class6-lab-challenge.md](class6-lab-challenge.md).

## Core finding
Pandemic-era air travel recovery was sharply uneven by airport *type*, not just by region:
- **Big-city hub airports** (SFO, Washington National, JFK) were still deeply depressed as of
  March 2021 — 24–46% of pre-pandemic (Feb 2020) volume at major West Coast hubs, National down
  70%, JFK around a third of normal.
- **Small vacation-oriented airports** near outdoor destinations were often *back to or above*
  pre-pandemic levels: Key West Int'l above 2019 levels; airports serving Yellowstone (WY, MT)
  fully recovered by early March 2021; Idaho Falls Regional (near Yellowstone) at 80% vs. Boise's
  ~60%; Canyonlands Field (UT, near Arches) back to normal; Northwest Florida Beaches up 24% y/y.
- **Exception that proves the "outdoors" pattern**: Hawaii's airports stayed deeply down
  (51–84% below normal) despite being an outdoor/vacation destination — attributed to strict
  entry rules (negative test within 3 days or 10-day quarantine) and longer flight times.

## Why the split — proposed mechanisms
1. **Trip purpose**: hub airports carry more business and international travel, both slower to
   recover than leisure travel.
2. **Local attractions closed**: urban draws (Broadway, Smithsonian) still shut, reducing reasons
   to fly into big cities specifically.
3. **Local risk tolerance varies by community**: Provo Municipal (UT) stayed busy — down only 2%
   y/y vs. Salt Lake City Int'l's 40% — driven by family visits and in-person BYU classes, not
   proximity to a national park. Willingness to travel during the pandemic differed city to city,
   independent of destination type.
